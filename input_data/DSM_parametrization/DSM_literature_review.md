# DSM demand-shiftability categorization plan

Cat 1 = fully flexible · Cat 2 = partially flexible, short timescales (clinker-style) · Cat 3 = not flexible at all. Pessimistic/optimistic columns are for running two scenarios.

| Sector | Pessimistic | Optimistic | Key source(s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glass | Cat 3 | Cat 3 | Lehigh IMI-NFG [1]; Refractory-Firebricks [2] |
| Ceramic | Cat 3 (continuous kilns) | Cat 2 (batch kilns) | Tangram [3] |
| Paper | Cat 2 | Cat 1 | Helin et al. 2017 [4] |
| Food | Cat 3 | Cat 2 | Interview conducted by Ana [5] |
| Methanol | Cat 2 | Cat 1 | AAU 2030 report [6]; Chen & Yang 2021 [7] |
| Primary steel | Cat 3 (BF-BOF, NG-DRI) | Cat 2 (H2-DRI-EAF) | Boldrini et al. 2024 [8]; Golmohamadi 2021 [9] |
| Secondary steel | Cat 2 | Cat 1 | Boldrini et al. 2024 [8]; Golmohamadi 2021 [9] |
| Olefin | Cat 3 (conventional cracker) | Cat 2 (electrified cracker) | ACS 2023 [10]; Mannhardt 2026 (unverified) |
| Ammonia | Cat 3 | Cat 2 | Salmon & Bañares-Alcántara 2023 [11]; Int. J. Hydrogen Energy 2025 [12] |
| Clinker | Cat 3 | Cat 3 | Golmohamadi 2021 [9] |

**Note:** while pulling exact BibTeX data from CrossRef I found [7] was mis-cited as *Applied Energy* in the earlier draft — it's actually published in *Energy Conversion and Management*. Corrected below.
